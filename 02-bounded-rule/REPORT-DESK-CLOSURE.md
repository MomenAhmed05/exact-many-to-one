# Closing the desk objective: an averaged-guarantee, data-computable threshold

Generated by `02-bounded-rule/make_desk_report.py` from the JSON deposits
under `02-bounded-rule/deposits/`. All tabulated figures are formatted from
deposit fields; the prose ranges below are computed from the same deposits.

## Headline

- **Guaranteed rule.** The exact-coverage envelope `c_env = max over the region of c*_pop` respects `FWER <= alpha + gamma` in the **averaged** sense in **12/12** desk-family cells, with the region-miss count 0 over 2400 draws. The same rule was measured on 66 grid cells (48 at K <= 3, 18 at K = 5/8): 0 bound violations, 1 pointwise-dominance violation of magnitude 1.6e-06 (K = 8, reduced candidate set: quadrature-level noise), the substantive pointwise violation being the K = 2 family-B draw at +0.0118 (section 1).
- **Price of the guarantee.** Power cost in `c` ranges **+1.58% to +22.99%** (mean +6.01%); the exact-coverage region buys that down from a union-bound mean of +7.24%.
- **Calibration arm (Track B).** Control variates cut the root-level noise of the calibration functional `F` by **2.33x** (antithetic pairing alone: 1.01x), and the end-to-end falsifier passes in 3/4 cases with the variance-reduced level at `B = 3000`, `D = 1200`; the PLAIN estimator fails at the same B (|z| up to 11.2).
- **Model layer (Track D).** Serial correlation and factor-common shocks are BOUNDARIES, not solved cases; the AR(1) boundary is stated for phi >= 0.4.

## 1. The guaranteed envelope (Phase A)

The two desk families are the ratio patterns **A** (`lambda = (0.04, 0.04)`, a
quiet benchmark) and **B** (`lambda = (1, 25)`, one identical candidate and one
25x-noisier candidate). Throughout, `alpha = 0.025` and `gamma = 0.0005`, so the
guarantee reads `FWER <= alpha + gamma = 0.0255`.

Write `c*_pop(lambda)` for the population exact critical value and `K` for the
number of candidate groups compared with the benchmark; the shape codes are
`balanced` (equal group sizes), `bench_small` (smaller benchmark groups) and
`short_cand` (smaller candidate groups), and the ratio codes are `noisy_bench`,
`noisy_cand` and `graded`.

The object is the maximum of the **population** exact critical value over a joint
confidence region for the variance ratios, built from the chi-square factorisation
of the estimated variances. The region is tightened so that its EXACT coverage
(1-D quadrature) equals `1 - gamma`, rather than relying on the union bound.

### What is certified and what is only measured

- Region coverage: exact, by 1-D quadrature, **0.999971 to 0.999998** against a target of `1 - gamma = 0.999500`.
- Monte Carlo coverage cross-check at a large gamma: 0.9902 vs target 0.90.
- Candidate-set check: random interior points never exceeded the candidate maximum (worst excess -1.7e-04).
- **Dominance is measured, not certified.** Over 1200 small-df draws the per-draw conditional rejection exceeded the population oracle on **1** draw(s); the averaged FWER estimate stayed inside the bound in all 6 cells.
- The `3^K` interior candidate set is **identical** to the corner set on these cells (worst c-deficit +0.0e+00), so the interior points are a safety margin, not a demonstrated necessity.

## 3. Track B -- variance-reduced calibration

The calibrated level `alpha*` is itself estimated from B inner worlds, so the
end-to-end falsifier is limited by that estimator noise. Two reductions are
tested: antithetic inner draws, and control variates with **exactly known**
means (`E[s2_0/sigma_0^2] = 1`, `E[log(s2_0/sigma_0^2)] = digamma(nu/2) +
log(2/nu)`), which keep the estimator unbiased even though beta is estimated.

| case | B | F_se ratio (VR) | F_se ratio (antithetic only) | control R^2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k2 S1 balanced, equal sd | 600 | 2.28x | 1.03x | 0.801 |
| k2 S1 balanced, equal sd | 1500 | 2.15x | 0.99x | 0.787 |
| k2 S1 balanced, equal sd | 3000 | 2.13x | 0.99x | 0.791 |
| k2 S4 control smallest n a | 600 | 2.44x | 1.03x | 0.829 |
| k2 S4 control smallest n a | 1500 | 2.38x | 1.01x | 0.818 |
| k2 S4 control smallest n a | 3000 | 2.38x | 1.01x | 0.826 |

Note: `F_se` is the root-level Monte Carlo standard error of the
calibration functional at the fitted root, computed on the SAME seeds for
both arms, so the ratio measures the noise reduction at the root; the
standard error of `alpha*` itself is `F_se / |F'(alpha*)|` and falls by
less, and the across-seed sd of `alpha*` with 4 seeds is too noisy to
resolve a factor-2 gain.

Cases passing `|z| < 3` with the variance-reduced alpha*: **3/4** at B = 3000, D = 1200.

Caveats (audited). `|z|` compares the residual to the OUTER Monte Carlo noise
only (D = 1200, mcse 5e-5..2e-4); the calibration error's own scale is the
across-seed sd of alpha* (3.24e-4..5.89e-4 at B = 3000), so the plain arm's S1
residual is roughly 1.3 calibration-sd rather than 11 sigma. The contrast is
still the plan's pass/fail gate, but the magnitudes must not be read as
calibration-error significance. The accept test calibrates once per (case,
variant) with a single seed, so it is one realisation.

**S5 multi-seed probe** (`deposits/calib_vr/s5_probe.json`). The single-seed S5 regression above is tested against 8 independent calibration seeds at B = 3000, D = 1200: VR residual mean 0.000800 (sd 0.001229), plain residual mean -0.000206 (sd 0.000969). Verdict: consistent with zero mean (mean +0.00080, t +1.8): the single B=3000 seed was unlucky rather than biased.

The seed-to-seed spread is the finding: at this B the single-realization
calibration error is large for BOTH arms, so a per-seed `|z|` gate is not a
meaningful acceptance test. What the control variates buy is a smaller
root-level noise (T5a's ratio), not a reliable single-run guarantee.
Two qualifications. The per-seed gate would have rejected 6/8 VR seeds and 3/8 plain seeds at `|z| = 3`. And the VR residual sd is actually the LARGER of the two here (ratio 1.27; with 8 seeds the interval on that ratio is wide), so the gain is at the root level rather than in the seed spread. The verdict rule uses a hard-coded `|t| <= 2.5` threshold, a script choice rather than a pre-registered criterion (at 2.0 the same data would read as bias), so read it as `not significant at n = 8` rather than `centred on zero`.

## 4. Track C -- the desk selector

Rule family (fixed in advance): **plug-in iff bench_df >= t AND ratio_spread <= s**. Split: random row-level, fixed seed (seed 20260916, 165 train / 165 test rows).

- train-fitted thresholds (`df >= 7`, spread <= 25): train accuracy **0.776** -> held-out **0.752**, routing 0.47 of rows to the guaranteed arm
- test-fitted thresholds (`df >= 11`, spread <= 25): held-out **0.764**
- where the train-fitted selector KEEPS the plug-in, the median error is **0.946%** (plan bar <= 1%), worst 7.784%
- legacy group-disjoint split (the first report's numbers, kept for traceability): train 0.783 / test 0.646 (thresholds df >= 5)
- desk families inside +/-2% under the plug-in: **False** (median over 4 seeds per cell; the robust outliers are the df-3 cells at 12-15%)

**Verdict: weak but usable, and the first report mis-stated it.** The earlier
held-out 0.646 came from a split that was group-disjoint by accident (every
design group landed wholly on one side); with a proper random row-level split
the same family reaches about 0.75-0.76, and where it keeps the plug-in the
median error is 0.95% against the plan bar of 1%. That is marginal, not
decisive: roughly a quarter of rows are misrouted and 47% go to the envelope
with its own power cost. Rows within a design group are dependent, so the
clustered standard error exceeds the binomial one. The envelope is now
measured at K = 5 and K = 8 too (section 2), but with weaker candidate sets
there, so 'use the envelope' carries the same caveat.

`phi <= 0.2` every deviation is within about 2.5 Monte Carlo sigma at R = 150, so
the boundary THERE is not resolved by this experiment. The block bootstrap is not
a rescue: it is mildly liberal at phi = 0 and any block length tested is at or
above alpha for phi >= 0.4. A double bootstrap or a calibrated level is left as
the boundary.

### One-factor candidate dependence (D2)

- loadings `same_sign`: FWER **0.0000** at the largest rho = 0.75 (alpha = 0.025, n = 36, draws = 2000).
- loadings `candidates_only`: FWER **0.0250** at the largest rho = 0.75 (alpha = 0.025, n = 36, draws = 2000).

## 6. Novelty positioning

- The *device* -- maximise a critical value over a confidence set for the
  nuisance -- is standard: Berger & Boos (1994), *P values maximized over a
  confidence set for the nuisance parameter*, JASA 89(427). Cite it, do not
  claim it.
- The exact many-to-one procedure at **known** variance ratios is prior art
  (JSTA 11(2), *A Multiple Comparison Procedure for Populations with Unequal
  Variances*).
- The estimated-ratio case has **simulation-based** competitors: multiplicity
  adjustments for the heteroscedastic Dunnett procedure (Biom. J. 2023,
  doi:10.1002/bimj.202200300) and a parametric-bootstrap MCC (J. Appl. Stat.
  2023, doi:10.1080/02664763.2023.2245179). Both are competitor descriptions
  read at abstract level, not full text.
- **What is new here**: an exact (non-simulation) critical value for the
  estimated-ratio problem with a *certified* slack limited to the miss budget
  `gamma`, plus an UNCERTIFIED sup-at-candidate step (1 violating draw in 1200,
  see section 7.1); a region certified by exact quadrature; a measured power
  price; and the desk-level map. No prior work was found that takes a supremum
  of an exact Dunnett critical value over a chi-square-factorised region (a
  bounded literature search, so absence is weak evidence).

## 7. What remains open

1. **Sup-at-candidate.** The guarantee needs `c_env` to attain the supremum over
   the region. No finite candidate set certifies that; dominance was measured
   (1 violating draw in 1200 small-df draws) and the averaged bound held
   everywhere. A Lipschitz or interval-arithmetic certificate would close this,
   or the guarantee must be stated as an averaged bound (as it is here).
2. **K = 5 and K = 8** are now measured (9 cells each) but with WEAKER candidate sets (2^K = 32 corners at K = 5, a reduced 3 + 2K = 19-point set at K = 8), and one pointwise-dominance violation appeared at K = 8, so the sup-at-candidate step remains measured rather than certified at every K.
3. **AR(1) and factor-candidate dependence** are stated boundaries, not solved
   cases; the AR(1) boundary below phi = 0.4 is unresolved.
4. **D2**: the corrected rule for known factor loadings is not implemented.
