## 1. S5.1 — Prior art, and the exact line the novelty sits on

Full detail in `PRIOR-ART.md`; machine-readable citations in `deposits/prior_art/prior_art.json`.
The short version, which is also the honest version:

| work | ratios | method | what it cannot deliver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dunnett (1955) | equal (known) | exact multivariate-t | anything under unequal variances |
| **Li (2009 diss. Ch. 2; = Li 2012 JSTA 11(2))** | **KNOWN `lambda`** | exact: condition on the control, pool `sigma_hat_0^2 = (1/v) sum_i S_i^2 (n_i-1)/lambda_i`, `v = sum(n_i-1)`, root a double integral | nothing for estimated ratios — the pooling is only legitimate *because* `lambda` is known |
| **Li (2009 diss. Ch. 6.3)** — closest prior art | **UNKNOWN `lambda`** | plug in the UMVU ratio estimator `lambda_hat_i = c S_i^2/S_0^2`, `c = (n_0-3)/(n_0-1)`, then fall back on **Bonferroni / Slepian conservative inequalities**, because the `z_i` correlation depends on the unknown variances and the k-fold integral is infeasible | an exact non-conservative critical value; any coverage accounting for the `lambda_hat - lambda` gap; any power-price statement |
| Tamhane & Xi (2023/24, Biom. J.) | unknown | correlated-chi-square approximation **generated by simulation** | determinism, exactness, a proof |
| JAS (2023) | unknown, unbalanced | **parametric bootstrap** | determinism, exactness, a proof |
| **this work** | **estimated `lambda_hat`** | tight chi-square region + population corner envelope + `gamma` budget; theorem-grade monotone-step certificate; measured price; desk map | the proof's price (+14…+34% family A, +133…+324% family B in `c`) and the envelope's `gamma` term are real costs |

**Why the line sits there.** Li's Ch. 6.3 shows the obstruction is structural, not technical: once
`lambda` is estimated, the standardised statistics are correlated through a matrix that is itself a
function of the unknown variances. The published responses are an inequality (valid, but pays in
power and restricts the correlation sign) or simulation (valid, but pays in determinism). This work
takes a third route — admit a ranked region with *certified* coverage `1 - gamma` and maximise the
exact critical value over it — so the `lambda_hat - lambda` gap appears as an explicit additive term
`P(lambda not in Lambda) <= gamma` instead of an inequality penalty.

**Claimed as novel:** an exact non-simulation critical value for the estimated-ratio problem; a
priced two-tier boundary (theorem-grade certificate vs measured envelope); the sup step measured on
a dense grid with the one violation attributed to a `gamma` event; the desk power-price table; the
AR(1) boundary and its negative effective-df result; a pre-registered calibration gate.
**Not claimed:** the device itself (maximise over a confidence region for the nuisance) is
Berger & Boos (1994), and the conditioning algebra is Li's.

## 2. S5.2 — The blind replication kit (`replicate/`)

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `replicate/METHOD.md` | complete method specification: the exact conditional law, the region and its exact coverage integral, the two rules, the 12 desk cells, the measurement protocol, and four falsifiers the replicator must run |
| `replicate/inputs/designs.json` | the 12 cells (family, df, n, `lambda_true`), the **actual per-cell seeds** copied from the deposits' `per_seed` records, `alpha`, `gamma`, quadrature settings, candidate-set rule, and the report template |
| `replicate/README.md` | how to run it, what to report, and the ground rules — including "do not read `../*.py` or `../deposits/**` before reporting your numbers" |

 **Independent replication (completed).** A separate implementation was written from `replicate/{README,METHOD,inputs}` alone, without access to the codebase, deposits or reports. It implemented the law from scratch with a *different* quadrature scheme (Gauss-Hermite in `U` plus composite Gauss-Legendre on `y = sqrt(X)` with a power map) and reproduced every **design-level** quantity, with a *sharp* rather than uniform agreement: family B matches the sealed deposits to four decimals in all six cells (B df3 2.7003 vs 2.7003, df5 2.5400 vs 2.5400, df7 2.4876 vs 2.4876, df11 2.4558 vs 2.4558, df19 2.4521 vs 2.4521, df35 2.4702 vs 2.4702), while family A agrees to the third-fourth decimal with a worst case of 9e-4 at A df3 (3.2192 vs 3.2201; A df35 2.1762 vs 2.1767, A df19 2.2095 vs 2.2100). An earlier draft called this "4-5 significant figures", which glossed A df3; the split is informative rather than a soft spot: A df3 is the numerically hardest corner (lowest df, widest region), where two different quadrature schemes are *expected* to part at their tolerances, and the B cells - whose integrands are better conditioned - agree exactly; all four falsifiers passed (F1 4.56e-9, F2 5.34e-11, F3 movement 0.0 on both axes, F4 z = +0.249 at N = 2e6, size 0.02503); paired dominance was `<= 0` in all 12 cells with `mean + 2SE <= 0` in 5/12 at n=200 (the rest being 1-2 discordant draws); and it independently reproduced the region-miss rate at B df3 (1/200) that the leak probe identified. It also exposed two specification defects in the kit, both now fixed: §1 conflated the conditional-on-variances law (oracle/certificate) with the unconditional law that F1/F2 refer to, and the draw order was not pinned - the reason its draw-level numbers agree only to the third digit while its design-level numbers agree to four decimals (family B) or three-to-four (family A). Reporting the defects is the point of the exercise: a kit that cannot be misread would not be testing anything.

**Outputs are withheld on purpose.** The kit gives the specification and the inputs; the expected
numbers stay sealed so the replication is a genuine test. Two deliberate design choices worth
noting:

1. **The seeds are given, not derived.** My first draft carried a reconstruction formula; checking it
   against `deposits/env/sweep_df*.json` showed the deposits store the *offsets* `[101,202,303,404]`
   while the rows carry the real seeds, so the kit was rewritten to copy the recorded per-cell seeds
   for all 12 cells. A formula that looked right but was off by the family index would have silently
   broken the draw-for-draw comparison — the kind of failure that makes a replication look like a
   discrepancy.
2. **The decisive quantity is paired dominance**, not the raw FWER: `P(T > c_env) - P(T > c_oracle)`,
   a per-draw difference. That is what makes the sup question answerable with 200 draws.

The kit also tells the replicator to validate the conditional law (F1) against the Student t
quantile *before* building anything, and to report F3 (grid refinement) as a failure rather than a
nuisance if it does not converge — because a non-converging quadrature would void the exactness
claim.
